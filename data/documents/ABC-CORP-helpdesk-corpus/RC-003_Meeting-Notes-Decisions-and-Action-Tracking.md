## 1. Before the meeting

- ☐ Confirm the meeting has a purpose. Write one sentence describing what the meeting must produce: a decision, an action, an approval, or information sharing. If the sentence is "information sharing," consider whether the information could be shared in writing.
- ☐ Confirm the required attendees. Distinguish required from optional. Only required attendees need to be present for the meeting to be valid.
- ☐ Send an agenda at least 24 hours before. The agenda lists each topic, the owner of the topic, and the time allocated. Agendas without times tend to run over.
- ☐ Book a note-taker. The meeting chair does not take notes. Assign a note-taker in advance.
- ☐ Prepare any materials. Materials for discussion should be sent at least 12 hours before. Materials sent at the start of the meeting reduce the meeting to a reading session.

## 2. During the meeting

- ☐ Start on time. Do not wait for late arrivals beyond 5 minutes.
- ☐ Confirm quorum. If a required attendee is absent, note it and consider whether the meeting can proceed.
- ☐ Follow the agenda. Time-box each topic. If a topic runs over, either extend its time by agreement or move it to a follow-up.
- ☐ Record decisions as decisions. A decision is a commitment to a course of action. Record:
  - ☐ The decision
  - ☐ The rationale (one sentence)
  - ☐ The decision-maker
  - ☐ The date
  - ☐ Any dissent
- ☐ Record actions as actions. An action is a task assigned to a person with a deadline. Record:
  - ☐ The action
  - ☐ The owner (a person, not a team)
  - ☐ The due date
  - ☐ The definition of done
- ☐ Do not record discussions that did not lead to decisions or actions. Discussion summaries are useful only if they explain a decision or an action. Otherwise, they create clutter and dilute the record.

## 3. After the meeting

- ☐ Send notes within 24 hours. Notes sent later than 24 hours are seldom read before the next meeting.
- ☐ Use the standard notes format:

```text
Meeting: [name]
Date: [date]
Attendees: [names, roles]
Absent: [names, required attendees only]
Chair: [name]
Note-taker: [name]

Decisions:
1. [Decision] — decided by [name] — rationale: [one sentence]

Actions:
| # | Action | Owner | Due | Definition of done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [action] | [name] | [date] | [done criteria] |

Next meeting: [date]
```

- ☐ Distribute the notes to all attendees and to anyone affected by the decisions or actions.
- ☐ Store the notes in the project workspace. Notes sent by email alone are not considered the record — the record is in the workspace, and email is notification.
- ☐ Add actions to the action tracker. The action tracker is a single project-level document. Actions tracked in email threads are not tracked.

## 4. Action tracking

Action trackers are reviewed at every meeting of the same group. The first agenda item of every recurring meeting is the review of open actions.

- ☐ For each open action:
  - ☐ Confirm the owner still owns it
  - ☐ Confirm the due date is still valid
  - ☐ If done, close it with the completion date
  - ☐ If late, either reset the due date with a reason or escalate
  - ☐ If no longer relevant, close it as "cancelled" with a reason

Actions more than two weeks overdue are escalated to the meeting chair for a decision: reset, reassign, or cancel.

Actions owned by people not present are not reset or reassigned without their agreement.

## 5. Decision log

Every project maintains a decision log, separate from meeting notes. The decision log captures decisions that affect the project outcome, regardless of which meeting they were made in. Meeting notes capture the meeting; the decision log captures the project.

The decision log format:

| Date | Decision | Decided by | Rationale | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [date] | [decision] | [name] | [one sentence] | [systems, teams, or scope affected] |

Decisions recorded in the decision log do not need to be re-litigated in later meetings unless new information emerges.

## 6. Meeting minutes retention

Meeting notes and decision logs are retained for:

- Project meetings — 3 years after project closure
- Governance meetings (board, executive, steering) — 7 years
- Operational meetings — 1 year

Retention is managed by the project owner or PMO per RC-001.

## 7. Meetings that are not minuted

Not every meeting requires minutes. Do not minute:

- Stand-ups and daily syncs
- Working sessions without decisions
- One-on-one meetings

Do minute:

- Any meeting where a decision is made
- Any meeting with external parties
- Any meeting where actions are assigned
- Any meeting that is part of a governance process

## 8. Tips for note-taking

- Type, do not write by hand, unless you type more slowly than you write
- Record the decision, then the discussion — not the other way around
- Use the attendee list from the calendar to avoid name errors
- If you are unsure what was decided, ask the chair at the meeting — not afterwards
- Do not edit notes after circulation without marking the edit
