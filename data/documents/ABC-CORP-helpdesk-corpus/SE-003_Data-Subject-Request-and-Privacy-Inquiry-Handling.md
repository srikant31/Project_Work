## What is a data subject request?

A data subject request (DSR) is any request from an individual — customer, employee, former employee, contractor, applicant, website visitor — to exercise rights over their personal data. The most common rights are:

- Access — "what data do you have about me?"
- Correction — "that data is wrong, fix it"
- Deletion — "delete my data"
- Portability — "give me my data in a usable format"
- Restriction — "stop processing my data while we sort this out"
- Objection — "stop using my data for this purpose"

Not every right applies in every jurisdiction. The Privacy Office determines applicability.

## What is a privacy inquiry?

A privacy inquiry is a question about how ABC CORP collects, uses, shares, or retains personal data. It is not a DSR but may become one — for example, "how long do you keep my data?" is an inquiry, but "delete my data" is a DSR.

## How to recognize a DSR

A DSR does not have to use legal language. Any of the following are DSRs:

- "I want to see everything you have on me"
- "Delete my account and all my data"
- "Stop emailing me"
- "Where did you get my information?"
- "I never agreed to this"
- Requests sent through a customer's account manager, through social media, or through a lawyer

If a request could reasonably be interpreted as exercising a privacy right, treat it as a DSR. Do not ask the requester to reframe it in legal terms.

## What to do when you receive a DSR

1. Do not answer it yourself. Even if the answer seems obvious.
2. Acknowledge receipt within 2 business days with a neutral response: "Thank you for your request. We have forwarded it to the appropriate team and will respond within the timeframe required by applicable law."
3. Log the request in the privacy request register. Include date received, requester name, contact information, channel, and a summary of the request.
4. Notify the Privacy Office by email with the register entry attached.
5. Preserve any relevant records. If the request relates to a specific interaction or transaction, do not delete or modify related records while the request is open.

## Verification

The Privacy Office verifies the requester's identity before fulfilling a DSR. Verification depends on the sensitivity of the data involved. For requests involving access to personal data, verification is typically two-factor. For deletion requests, verification confirms the requester is entitled to request deletion.

Do not tell the requester what verification steps will be used — the Privacy Office handles verification communication directly.

## Timeframes

The Privacy Office responds within the timeframe required by applicable law. In most jurisdictions this is 30 days from receipt, with an extension possible in some cases. The Privacy Office tracks every request against its statutory deadline.

Service Desk's responsibility ends when the request is logged and the Privacy Office is notified. Do not commit to a response date with the requester.

## Common categories of requests

**Customer requests** — most DSRs come from customers. Route to the Privacy Office; the Privacy Office coordinates with the customer's account team.

**Employee and former employee requests** — route to the Privacy Office; HR Operations is copied. Employee DSRs are handled by the Privacy Office with HR involvement for records access.

**Job applicant requests** — route to the Privacy Office. Applicant data is held in the recruiting system.

**Marketing opt-out requests** — these are handled by the marketing operations team and are not logged as DSRs unless combined with another request.

## What not to do

- Do not confirm or deny what data ABC CORP holds about the requester
- Do not delete any records while a request is open
- Do not forward the request to a business unit without copying the Privacy Office
- Do not respond to the requester on social media
- Do not assume a request is invalid because it comes from a third party — a lawyer or authorized agent may submit on the requester's behalf

## If the request comes from a regulator

Regulator inquiries are not DSRs. Route any communication from a data protection authority to the Legal department immediately. Do not respond to the regulator directly.
